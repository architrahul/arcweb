# PPH Cup Device Brief

## Positioning

PPH Cup is a low-cost silicone medical device for quantitative postpartum hemorrhage monitoring. It is designed to capture post-delivery blood loss, route it through tubing, and collect it in a graduated bag so obstetric teams can act on measured volume rather than visual estimation alone.

The device should be presented as a monitoring and early-recognition tool. It supports clinical decision-making during the postpartum hemorrhage golden hour; it does not replace uterotonics, resuscitation, transfusion, tamponade, surgery, or clinician judgment.

## Clinical Context

- Postpartum hemorrhage is commonly defined as blood loss of 500 mL or more within 24 hours after birth.
- Severe PPH is commonly defined as blood loss of 1,000 mL or more, or bleeding associated with hemodynamic instability.
- ACOG defines maternal hemorrhage as cumulative blood loss of 1,000 mL or more, or blood loss with signs or symptoms of hypovolemia within 24 hours after birth, regardless of route of delivery.
- WHO's 2025 diagnostic/action threshold is objective blood loss of at least 300 mL plus any abnormal hemodynamic sign, or objective blood loss of at least 500 mL, whichever occurs first within 24 hours.
- Pregnancy circulating blood volume is often approximated at 100 mL/kg; for a 60 kg pregnant patient, 500 mL is about 8.3%, 1,000 mL is about 16.7%, 1,500 mL is about 25%, and 2,400 mL is about 40% of estimated blood volume.
- Pregnant patients can lose more than 1,000 mL before signs of hypovolemia are apparent; signs may correspond to more than 25% blood-volume loss.
- The obstetric hemorrhage golden hour is the first 60 minutes after onset or recognition of hemorrhage, when recognition, resuscitation, treatment escalation, and prevention of shock are time-critical.
- WHO's Roadmap cites about 14 million PPH cases and about 70,000 maternal deaths globally each year.
- WHO's maternal mortality fact sheet reports about 260,000 global maternal deaths in 2023, with 92% occurring in low- and lower-middle-income countries.
- India's latest official SRS maternal mortality ratio is 88 deaths per 100,000 live births for 2021-2023.
- With roughly 23-25 million births annually, India has about 20,000-22,000 maternal deaths per year.
- Depending on the attribution source used, PPH or obstetric hemorrhage may account for roughly 4,000-9,500 maternal deaths annually in India.
- Visual estimation of obstetric blood loss is frequently inaccurate; ACOG cites about 30% error, and studies report underestimation at high volumes in the 30-50% range.
- WHO recommends routine objective measurement of postpartum blood loss for all women giving birth.
- WHO recommends IV tranexamic acid within 3 hours of birth for women with PPH.
- An early-detection plus treatment-bundle strategy reduced severe bleeding by about 60% in a trial of more than 200,000 women.

## Device Design

- Soft silicone cup for placement in the lower or mid vaginal canal after delivery, or around caesarean-section workflow as clinically directed.
- Direct transfer stem and tubing route collected blood to a graduated collection bag.
- Secondary port supports intermittent suction or patency protocols when clinically needed.
- User guidelines describe suction every 15-30 minutes for the first 1-2 hours, then less frequently according to protocol.
- Device cleaning options in the user-guideline archive include cleaning solution, spirit, formalin storage, Cidex, or autoclaving; the notes caution that frequent autoclaving may harden silicone.

## Clinical-Study Signals

The thesis archive summarizes an 18-month observational study at Government Medical College, Akola, with 300 participants.

- 300 total cases.
- 64% pre-placement easy acceptance; 36% accepted after persuasion.
- 92% post-placement acceptance with no discomfort.
- 0% refusal rate.
- 95% reported no fever; no local infection was reported.
- 2.67% of monitored cases showed alarming blood loss indicating PPH within one hour.
- 100% timely alarm and accurate post-delivery blood-loss measurement was reported.
- Among detected PPH cases, 50% required blood transfusion and 50% did not.
- Mechanical compression devices were required in 62.5% of detected PPH cases.
- 75% of detected PPH cases did not require invasive surgical intervention.
- 37.5% of detected PPH cases required ICU admission.
- 75% of detected PPH cases had reduced maternal morbidity attributable to PPH.
